# Microsoft Exchange Online intrusion (Storm-0558) (2023)

**Topic:** cloud, identity  
**Level:** intermediate  
**Last checked:** 2026-11-22

## What happened

In May and June 2023, a China-linked group called Storm-0558 got into the Exchange Online mailboxes of 22 organizations and more than 500 people, including senior U.S. officials. [KNOWN: CSRB MEO review]

## How they got in and what went wrong

- They forged login tokens using a Microsoft signing key created in 2016. [KNOWN: CSRB]
- Combined with another flaw, the key let them reach essentially any Exchange Online account. [KNOWN: CSRB]
- As of the report, Microsoft did not know how or when the key was stolen, and a customer, not Microsoft, first spotted the anomaly. [KNOWN: CSRB]

## ATT&CK techniques involved

- [T1606](https://attack.mitre.org/techniques/T1606/) Forge Web Credentials
- [T1078](https://attack.mitre.org/techniques/T1078/) Valid Accounts
- [T1114](https://attack.mitre.org/techniques/T1114/) Email Collection

Mapping: [INFERRED] our reading of the sources, using ATT&CK v20.1 names. Advisories from CISA often include their own official mapping; check the source for theirs.

## Lessons

- Signing keys are crown jewels. Rotate them and protect them in hardware.
- Good logging matters. The customer caught this because it had the right logs turned on.
- The Board called the intrusion preventable and said Microsoft's security culture needed an overhaul. [KNOWN: CSRB]
